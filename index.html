Safeguarding & ethical recruitment

The people we place often work with vulnerable communities. How we recruit matters as much as who we recruit.

01

Safeguarding in every reference check

We ask referees directly about conduct and safeguarding, and we support clients who use the inter-agency Misconduct Disclosure Scheme to check a candidate’s history with previous employers.

02

Fair and equal assessment

We assess candidates only on job-relevant skills and experience, never on gender, race, religion, nationality, disability, age or other personal characteristics. Our AI tools follow the same rule, and a person makes every decision.

03

National and local talent first

We actively search for national professionals and leaders, supporting the localization commitments of our clients and their donors.

04

Fair to candidates

Candidates never pay us a fee. We share a CV with an employer only with the candidate’s permission, and we keep candidates informed at every stage.

05

Data handled with care

CVs are stored securely with access limited to our recruitment team, and we delete a candidate’s data whenever they ask. Read our privacy notice.

▶How do your fees work?

We work on contingency: you pay only when you hire a candidate we introduced. The fee is a percentage of the hire’s first-year base salary, agreed in writing before we start. For larger or multi-role projects, we can agree an arrangement that fits your budget.

▶What if a hire doesn’t work out?

If a candidate we placed leaves or is let go within 90 days of starting, we run a replacement search at no extra fee.

▶How long does a search take?

Most clients receive a shortlist within 2–3 weeks. Surge hiring and proposal deadlines can move faster. Executive and board-level searches usually take longer because the assessment goes deeper.

▶Do you work with small and national NGOs?

Yes. We work with organizations of every size, from global INGOs and U.S. government implementing partners to national NGOs and U.S. community nonprofits, and we scale our support to your budget and timeline.

▶Can you find key personnel for a proposal?

Yes. We identify and vet named candidates, prepare CVs in the donor’s format, collect letters of commitment and biodata forms, and keep candidates engaged until the award decision so they’re still available when you need them.

▶Where do you recruit?

Worldwide. We recruit national staff in-country and international staff for roles across the Middle East and North Africa, Africa, Latin America, Europe & Eurasia and Asia, as well as headquarters and U.S.-based roles.

▶How do you use AI?

AI helps us search our network and read CVs faster. It never makes a hiring decision: a recruiter reviews every candidate, interviews and checks references. Candidates are assessed only on job-relevant skills and experience. See our privacy notice for how we handle data.

▶What happens after I get in touch?

We set up a short call to understand the role, the program and your timeline. Then we send a written proposal with the scope, fee and timeline. No work starts, and nothing is owed, until you agree to it.

    Prepare for your interview

    Interviews for donor-funded roles test three things: can you do the technical work, can you do it in this context, and will you manage donor requirements well. Prepare for all three.

    1 · Research the program

    • Read the job description twice and highlight the must-haves.
    • Learn the program’s goals, funder, location and stage (proposal, start-up, ongoing).
    • Look up the organization’s recent work in the country.

    2 · Prepare your stories

    • Have 5–6 examples ready, built with the STAR method below.
    • Cover results, a challenge, teamwork, a mistake and leadership.
    • Put numbers in every story.

    3 · Know the donor side

    • Be ready to discuss the donor rules you’ve worked under.
    • For key personnel roles, expect questions on availability and references.
    • Have your employment dates and salary history accurate.

    4 · Plan the logistics

    • Confirm the time zone. Many panels span several countries.
    • Test your video, sound and connection; keep a phone backup.
    • Prepare two or three questions to ask the panel.

    Questions to ask the panel

    1. What would success look like in the first six months?
    2. What are the biggest challenges facing the program right now?
    3. How does the team work with local partners and government counterparts?
    4. How is this role supported: supervision, learning, team structure?
    5. What are the next steps and timeline? For proposals, ask when the award decision is expected.

    Day-before checklist

    Interviewing for a key personnel or proposal role

    • You may be named in a proposal. Organizations often ask candidates to sign a letter of commitment confirming they’ll be available if the award is made. Only commit to proposals you would really accept.
    • Expect a biodata form. Many U.S. government-funded proposals require a signed form listing your employment history and past salaries. Keep accurate dates and pay records ready.
    • References get checked early. Tell your referees they may be contacted, and choose people who saw your work on similar programs.
    • Timelines can be long. Award decisions can take months. Ask about the expected timeline, and keep us updated if your availability changes.

    CV tips for development professionals

    What recruiters for NGOs, INGOs and donor-funded programs look for when they read your CV.

    resultsLead with results, not duties

    Hiring managers want to know what changed because of your work. Start each line with an action and end with a result.

    Responsible for monitoring activities.
    Designed the MEL plan for a $12M education program, tracking 18 indicators across 300 schools.

    donorsName the donors and award types

    Experience with a specific funder is often a must-have. Say who funded the program and, where relevant, whether it was a grant, cooperative agreement or contract.

    Grants Manager, USAID-funded health program (cooperative agreement, $28M)

    contextShow where you’ve worked

    Country and regional experience matters. List countries in each role, and add a short “countries worked in” line near the top.

    languagesBe precise about languages

    Write a clear level for each language: native, fluent, professional working, or basic. If you’ve taken a formal test, include the score.

    keywordsMatch the job description

    Recruiters and screening tools look for the must-haves in the job description. Use the same terms where they honestly describe your experience, for example “MEL” or “sub-award management”.

    lengthTwo to four pages is normal

    Development CVs are usually longer than corporate resumes. Keep the last 10–15 years detailed and summarize earlier roles. Some donors require their own CV format for key personnel; we’ll tell you when that applies.

    formatKeep the layout simple

    Use clear headings, standard fonts and no photos, tables or text boxes. Simple layouts read correctly in applicant tracking systems. Send PDF unless Word is requested.

    accuracyGet dates and titles exactly right

    Employment dates, titles and salaries are often verified for donor-funded roles. Make sure your CV matches what your references and records will show.
